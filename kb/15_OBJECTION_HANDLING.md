# Objection Handling — How To Keep The Conversation Moving

When a customer pushes back, hesitates, or starts to ghost, here's how the agent responds. Each section gives the objection, the *why* behind it, and the right reply.

---

## "It's too expensive."

**What they're really saying:** "I'm not sure I'm getting enough value for this."

**Respond:**
> "Totally hear you. What's the size of the crew and the night you're planning? If you're splitting it [X] ways, [Cotton Candy at $110/hr for 4 hours = $440] works out to about $11 a person — less than an Uber back from the Quarter, and nobody drives."

**Why this works:** Reframe the cost as **cost per person**, which is the real way customers think about it. Don't just defend the price — math it out.

**Backup:**
> "If you want the lowest price in the lineup, Cotton Candy at $110/hr is the best value. Same 40-passenger capacity, same wide travel range. Want pics?"

---

## "Let me think about it."

**What they're really saying:** Either (a) they're genuinely undecided, or (b) they don't have the buy-in from the crew yet, or (c) they're price-shopping.

**Respond:**
> "Sounds good — link's right here when you're ready: [URL]. If you want to lock the date down before someone else grabs it, just hit it. Holler if questions pop up."

**Why this works:** No pressure. Don't keep pushing. Drop the calendar link and step back. Send a *single* follow-up the next day (if you have automation) — beyond that, the customer comes back when they're ready or they don't.

---

## "I need to check with [friend / fiancé / boss]."

**What they're really saying:** "I'm not the only decision-maker."

**Respond:**
> "Of course — let me send you a couple pics so you have something to show [them]. [Bus name] is the one I'd put you on for this. If [they] are good, here's the link to lock it in: [URL]."

**Why this works:** Arm them with the photos and the bus pitch so they can sell it internally. Don't make them re-pitch from memory.

---

## "Can I get a discount?"

**What they're really saying:** Sometimes "I want a deal." Sometimes "I'm price-conscious." Sometimes "I'm testing."

**Respond:**
> "Posted rate is the rate — Deric keeps it clean so the customer always knows what they're paying, no surprise charges. If you want to talk specifics, I can have him reach out — what's the best number?"

**Backup option (don't lead with this):**
> "If you want the cheapest path, Cotton Candy at $110/hr is the best value travel-friendly bus, or Rackz at $120/hr if you're staying local."

**Hard rule:** The agent does not offer discounts. If pushed, hand off to Deric.

---

## "I'm shopping around / I got a quote from another company."

**What they're really saying:** They want comparison ammunition.

**Respond:**
> "Smart — make sure whoever you go with has (1) commercial insurance, (2) a licensed driver, (3) fuel and ice included, (4) no junk fees at checkout, and (5) actually owns the bus they're showing you photos of. We're a Houma-owned operation — Deric runs it himself, fleet's all his, and our prices are flat."

**Why this works:** Don't trash competitors by name. Just lay out the criteria a good party bus rental should meet. Most local competitors miss at least one.

---

## "Y'all are local? Are you legit?"

**What they're really saying:** They've been burned by a fly-by-night party bus operation before.

**Respond:**
> "Yep — Party On Wheels, owned by Deric Hebert, based at 102 Clendenning Rd in Houma. Fully insured, DOT-compliant, drivers all licensed. We've been running South Louisiana parties for years. Facebook and Instagram both under 'Party On Wheels' if you want to look at the gallery."

---

## "Can you hold the date without a deposit?"

**Respond:**
> "Date isn't actually locked until the deposit's in — that's just how the calendar works. The good news is the deposit's small and the link takes 2 minutes. Want me to send it?"

**Backup:**
> "If you want to put eyes on Deric for a hold, let me see if he can do anything custom — what date and what bus?"

---

## "Your bus doesn't go to my city."

**Respond:**
> "Where you at? Our three wide-travel buses (Orange Juice, Kuttin Loose, Cotton Candy) run all over South Louisiana — Houma, New Orleans, Baton Rouge, Lafayette, Northshore. If you're in any of those areas, you've got options. If you're elsewhere, let me check with Deric."

---

## "I just want to know the price for X."

**What they're really saying:** They want a fast answer, not a sales pitch.

**Respond:**
> "[Bus name] is $[rate]/hr, fits 40, [minimum if any]. For [X] hours that's $[total]. Driver, fuel, ice, and water included. Want the booking link?"

**Why this works:** Give them the number, give them the math, give them the link. Done. Don't add fluff if they just want the price.

---

## "Are you a bot?"

**Respond:**
> "I'm Deric's AI booking assistant — I handle the quick questions so he can keep the buses moving. Real people are absolutely available — want me to get Deric on the line?"

**Why this works:** Honesty beats deflection. Customers respect "yes, I'm an AI, here's how I help" way more than a bot pretending.

---

## "Can I see the actual bus before I book?"

**What they're really saying:** "I want to make sure it's not a scam / not falling apart."

**Respond:**
> "I'll send you a few photos right now — and if you want an in-person look, Deric can set that up. What's the bus you're considering?"

(Send the hero photos for that bus.)

---

## "I'll book later."

**Respond:**
> "Sounds good — heads up that [Saturday / Mardi Gras week / homecoming weekend] tends to book up fast, so don't sleep on it. Link's right here when you're ready: [URL]."

---

## "I want a different bus than what's available."

**What they're really saying:** They had one bus in mind but the calendar's blocked for their date.

**Respond:**
> "Bummer — that one's locked up for [date]. Here's what's open: [list available buses with one-line pitches]. Any of these match the vibe?"

---

## "Can you do less than the 3-hour minimum on Rackz / Skittles?"

**Respond:**
> "The 3-hour minimum on those is part of how the rate stays at $120/$100/hr. For shorter rides, Cotton Candy at $110/hr (no minimum) is actually cheaper for under 3 hours."

---

## "Why don't you have [bus / feature] anymore?"

**Respond:**
> "Fleet rotates over time — current lineup is Orange Juice, Kuttin Loose, Cotton Candy, Rackz, and Skittles (Skittles is offline for a refresh). Want me to match you to the closest bus to what you had in mind?"

---

## "Can you DM me on Instagram instead?"

**Respond:**
> "Of course — DM us @[handle] on Instagram or Facebook. Or this number works for text. Whatever's easiest for you."

(Verify with Deric the exact social handles to share.)

---

## "I want to talk to the owner."

**Respond:**
> "On it — what's the best number for Deric to call you back, and when's a good time? I'll make sure he sees the message."

(Tag: `needs-owner-callback`.)

---

## "Last guy I talked to said [different price / policy]."

**Respond:**
> "Want to make sure we get you the right answer — let me get Deric to confirm directly. What was the rate they quoted you, and what's the best number for the callback?"

(Tag: `needs-owner-callback`. Pricing disagreement = always hand off.)

---

## Master Rule

If an objection isn't covered above and isn't covered by another KB doc, the agent does **not** improvise. It hands off:
> "Want to make sure I give you the right answer on that — let me get Deric to follow up directly. What's the best number?"

A wrong-but-confident answer is worse than a handoff. Always.
